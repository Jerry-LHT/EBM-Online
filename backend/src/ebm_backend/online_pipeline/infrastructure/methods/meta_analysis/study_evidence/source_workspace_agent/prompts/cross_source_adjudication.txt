Adjudicate one article's already verified source-local evidence cards against
the frozen synthesis targets. The raw sources were read independently. Do not
invent missing context, recalculate values or treat an ID as evidence; use each
card's exact source scope, quote, header path, footnote links, arm identity and
uncertainty.

Select a complete supported result representation for each target. Evidence
from multiple sources may be combined only when it describes the same
article-local outcome, measure, timepoint, result frame, analysis population and
article arm. A denominator or participant-flow material from another source is
supporting evidence; it does not change the candidate table's outcome identity.
For multi-arm trials select the actual eligible StudyMap arm IDs required by the
frozen comparison.

The payload lists alternative supported result representations. Select exactly
one complete representation for a confirmed or corrected verdict. In the direct
continuous representation, select one `direct_effect` card and one matching
`direct_uncertainty` card. The uncertainty card may contain a reported SE or a
reported confidence interval; deterministic code derives the final SE. Do not
reuse one evidence ID for two fields and do not mix an incomplete arm-level
representation into a complete direct-effect representation.

Every verdict must return `direct_effect_semantics`. For a dichotomous target
or a continuous arm-level representation, mark its comparison direction,
change-score direction, basis and confidence `not_applicable`, with a concise
rationale. For a continuous direct effect, provide the working comparison
direction and, when the result is a change score, the working change-score
direction. These are the directions
used for deterministic normalization; they are not required to be literal
author wording. If the article does not state the subtraction order, you may
infer a working interpretation only from the grounded evidence in this
payload: unique arm identity, the named scale's clinical direction, same-result
arm summaries, and an explicit article interpretation such as which group
improved more. Record `basis: "cross_source_inference"`, confidence, and a
concise rationale describing the evidence and any equivalent interpretation.
Do not infer a direction from treatment expectations alone, and do not invent
or recalculate the reported effect magnitude. If the evidence does not support
a stable working direction, mark the direct-effect semantics insufficient and
return an `unresolved` verdict instead of assigning a default sign.

When the same-result arm summaries establish a clinically directional contrast,
they can resolve an otherwise unordered direct label. For example, if a named
symptom scale is higher-is-worse and the experimental arm has the more negative
post-minus-baseline change, a valid working pair is
`control_minus_experimental` with `post_minus_baseline`; the algebraically
equivalent `experimental_minus_control` with `baseline_minus_post` is also
valid. Choose one pair, explain the arm-level and scale-direction evidence, and
record `basis: "cross_source_inference"`. This uses the summaries only to
interpret orientation; it must not replace, recompute, or numerically validate
the reported direct estimate.

Generic role wording such as control, intervention, treatment, experimental or
comparator does not establish arm identity. Rely on the StudyMap canonical arm,
explicit aliases, regimen descriptions and the source-local observed label. Do
not merge distinct arms merely because they share a role. Do not split one arm
merely because different tables use supported aliases.

Choose candidate evidence by semantic fit and source scope, never by effect
magnitude, significance or convenience. Once one representation has been
selected, its sign may be interpreted using the named scale and an explicit
same-result clinical interpretation; this is direction normalization, not a
reason to select a more favorable candidate. Distinguish a hard semantic conflict
from incomplete scope documentation. If the numeric fields form one defensible
representation and the remaining uncertainty is limited to an incompletely
documented analysis population, denominator linkage or exact timepoint label,
select the best-supported evidence and record the limitation as an assumption
or competing interpretation. Retain the evidence card's low or medium
confidence and incomplete or requires_audit scope; deterministic code marks the
affected final fields as provisional. Return unresolved only when no defensible
representation exists, a required numeric field is absent, evidence is
semantically incompatible, or genuinely tied interpretations remain after
comparing the verified cards. Reference evidence_ids only; the system copies
the grounded materials and performs deterministic validation and all
arithmetic.

For a continuous target, also classify the clinical direction of its named
scale. Use source_reported only when the article evidence states which direction
is better or worse. Use expert_inference when a clearly identified standard
instrument has a well-established direction even though this article does not
repeat it; explain the instrument-based reasoning and do not present it as an
article quote. Use insufficient_information when the scale, version or direction
cannot be established reliably. For a dichotomous target use not_applicable.
Scale direction is clinical interpretation metadata: an unknown direction does
not by itself make a post-intervention experimental-minus-control MD
statistically unavailable. It remains essential when opposite-direction scales
or change-score subtraction orders must be harmonized.

Reconcile source-local randomized-arm observations into the actual article-level
study arms. Each observation is grounded in one table and may also be used by
one or more result candidates. Return an explicit partition: every observation
must belong to exactly one canonical arm or be listed as unresolved.

Arm identity is semantic, not a normalized-string or role match. Control,
treatment, intervention, experimental, comparator, Group A and similar generic
words do not establish identity by themselves. Merge observations only when
the article evidence supports the same regimen through intervention content,
drug/device/procedure, dose, frequency, duration, explicit abbreviation or code
mapping, unique trial structure, or another concrete identity relation. Do not
merge placebo, usual care, no treatment and an active comparator merely because
all have a control role. Do not merge distinct doses or regimens merely because
all have an experimental role.

Conversely, different table labels should be grouped when the article supports
that they name the same actual arm. Choose a specific canonical label that
describes the regimen. Preserve all source labels as aliases. Distinct real arms
must remain separate even if the review may later combine them statistically.

The investigator StudyMap and semantic notes may help interpret source-local
observations, but they are not authority over the raw-source observations.
Provide concrete evidence source refs and a concise rationale for every group.
If identity cannot be established uniquely, leave the observation unresolved;
do not guess. Do not calculate effects or select synthesis results. Engineering
code validates the partition and assigns stable arm IDs.

# Authority Discovery And Sufficiency

## Distinguish discovery from execution

An official page may establish only that a method exists or identify its
current version. Continue to the material that contains the operational
content needed by the parent task. Depending on the method, useful source roles
may include:

- an identity, release, or update page that establishes version and variant;
- operative guidance explaining applicability and professional procedure;
- an instrument, template, algorithm, technical supplement, or platform guide
  needed to perform or verify the method;
- a correction, update notice, or archived version relevant to a
  Protocol-bound choice.

These roles are not a required document checklist. One source may fill several
roles, or the relevant guidance may be distributed across several official
pages. Judge sufficiency against the parent's actual execution need.

## Test whether the material is usable

Before handing authority to the parent, be able to answer from inspected
content:

- Why is this method, version, and variant applicable to this task?
- Which sections, questions, branches, calculations, or decisions must be
  executed now?
- What makes a branch applicable or not applicable, and how must that state be
  represented?
- What output, explanation, provenance, or quality check does the method
  require?
- Which relevant details remain unavailable or ambiguous?

If the answers come from memory while the cited page only names the method,
the authority has not been read deeply enough.

## Adapt to source and access conditions

- A version page links a full guide or implementation tool: use the page to
  establish identity, then inspect the linked execution-bearing material.
- An online handbook chapter contains the complete guidance needed for the
  current decision: record the exact chapter or sections used; do not require a
  separate PDF merely for format consistency.
- A method relies on an official platform's current syntax or export behavior:
  inspect the platform documentation needed for that operation in addition to
  general methodological guidance.
- Guidance is spread across a living manual: inspect the pages governing the
  current decision and record the access date and actual scope rather than
  inventing a version.
- A PDF or page is inaccessible or unreadable: try a materially different
  official representation, archive supplied by the authority, rendered view,
  or another primary source that can resolve the same need. Do not bypass
  access controls.

Search snippets, bibliographic citations, landing-page descriptions, training
summaries, and third-party explanations may identify useful leads. They do not
replace directly applicable official or primary execution guidance when that
guidance is available.

## Hand off without a new artifact

Use the parent task's existing fields. Record direct locators rather than only
the site homepage, name the version or revision honestly, identify what was
actually read, connect professional decisions to the relevant authority, and
state limitations. Do not copy full manuals, templates, or tools into the task
artifact.

An unresolved authority gap is a methodology-sufficiency limitation, not task
incompletion by itself. If the parent can make and record a coherent,
executable decision (including an explicit `llm_fallback` when permitted),
finish the task as `completed` with a warning. Reserve `partial` for required
professional work that remains undone, and `blocked` for the absence of a
valid minimum output. Do not mechanically aggregate authority access into the
task status.

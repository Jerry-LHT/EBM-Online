# Evidence And Task Boundary

Selection owns eligibility, Study identity, Report identity, and authoritative
Study–Report association. Read `study-decisions.jsonl`, `studies.jsonl`,
`reports.jsonl`, and `study-report-links.jsonl` from the verified package.
Mirror those identifiers exactly in the collection document.

Do not create, confirm, or modify a Study–Report link. If reading reveals a
possible unlinked companion Report or a material identity problem, record a
bounded upstream issue with the discovery evidence. Do not use the candidate
as authoritative Study evidence until Selection supplies a verified link.

An abstract, citation, snippet, landing page, complete Report, registry record,
posted registry result, supplement, correction, and unavailable route have
different evidentiary scope. Record what was actually read. A complete
registry record may support planned design and any results it actually posts,
but it is not a complete journal Report. A complete Report claim requires
inspection of the content needed by this task, not merely opening a URL or
finding a PDF link.

Do not infer a complete registry record from the Search Record type or a
Selection evidence label. The persisted Search representation may omit
registry modules. Inspect the current registry representation and identify the
protocol, status, outcome, and posted-results modules actually read before
making a scope claim. Absence of a posted-results module after that inspection
can support `unreported`; absence from an unverified snapshot cannot.

For each Protocol-required data need, judge whether the currently read source
actually answers it. When a registry-only source leaves a material need open,
investigate credible lawful linked or companion Reports before recording the
field as unavailable. When no such source can be found or read after a
case-adapted investigation, retain the registry-derived information, identify
the missing need and actual read scope, and record the field as unreported or
unavailable. Do not treat that local limitation as a task failure.

Keep source access and item reporting separate throughout extraction. The fact
that a Report was inspected does not mean every requested item was reported;
the fact that an item is absent from the current document does not establish
that the Report failed to report it until the relevant representation was
actually read. An outcome listed in a registry or Protocol is an outcome
definition or plan, not a Study Result. Conversely, a qualitative or
categorical finding is reported content even when no RevMan numeric
representation is possible.

Do not conclude from an abstract that a complete journal Report lacks numeric
Results. When the task needs arm-level values, inspect the relevant body text,
tables, figures, footnotes, and supplements in a readable representation. If
that inspection remains impossible, preserve the abstract finding and record
the actual partial scope and unresolved numeric need.

Store concise evidence descriptions and provenance, never full text. Preserve
not-reported, unavailable, not-applicable, conflicting, and uncertain states;
do not fill them from the Protocol or model recollection.

Protocol outcomes define what to seek; they are not evidence that a Study
measured or omitted those outcomes. Do not create repeated source-only Results
from the Protocol alone. Every collected Result state must point to the Report
coverage and investigation that supports it, while a genuinely uninvestigated
need remains unfinished work.

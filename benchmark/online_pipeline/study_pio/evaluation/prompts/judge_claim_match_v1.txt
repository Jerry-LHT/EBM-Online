Judge Study-level PIO semantic claim coverage for one included clinical study.

Context:
- The gold text is the Cochrane-style reference study characteristic for this included study.
- The predicted text is a method output for the same study.
- The question PICO is context only. It helps interpret abbreviations and outcome domains, but it is not itself a gold label.

For each field, split the gold and predicted text into concise, atomic semantic claims. Match a predicted claim to a gold claim when they describe the same study-level population, intervention/comparator, or outcome concept.

General matching rules:
- Allow synonyms, abbreviations, spelling variants, and reasonable summarization.
- Keep gold_claims and predicted_claims brief. Paraphrase each claim in your own words; do not copy long source passages.
- For very long outcome lists, group repeated variants under the same outcome domain and time window when the distinction would not change matching.
- Match a more specific predicted claim to a broader gold claim if the clinical/study concept is the same.
- Match a broader predicted claim to a more specific gold claim only if it preserves the key distinguishing detail.
- Do not create extra unmatched predicted claims from harmless qualifiers, wording differences, or measurement detail that is nested inside an already matched claim.
- Mark an unmatched predicted claim only when it asserts a distinct material detail not covered by any gold claim.
- Do not match wrong population, wrong arm/comparator, wrong outcome domain, or unsupported expansion that changes meaning.

Field-specific rules:
- population: match eligibility criteria, setting/country, sample size, arm sizes, age/sex, disease severity, and important baseline characteristics. Do not over-penalize enrolled/randomized/analyzed sample-size wording when they refer to the same study population.
- intervention_comparator: split intervention and comparator arm details. Match arm content, dose, duration, frequency, provider/supervision, setting, co-interventions, and decision-aid components when semantically equivalent.
- outcomes: match outcome domains, measurement instruments/scales, adverse effects/harms, timepoints, and follow-up windows. Review-relevant outcome domains may be broader than the exact article wording. Do not match process/implementation/economic outcomes to clinical outcomes unless the gold explicitly includes that domain.
